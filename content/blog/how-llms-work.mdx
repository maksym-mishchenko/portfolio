## Tokenization: Text → Numbers

Before a model can process your text, it needs to convert words into numbers. This process is called **tokenization**.

Models don't work with whole words — they break text into **subword tokens** using Byte Pair Encoding (BPE). Here's how "San Francisco" gets tokenized:

<Tokenizer
  text="San Francisco"
  tokens='["▁San", "▁Franc", "isco"]'
  tokenIds='[3051, 12468, 6245]'
/>

Hit play ▶ to see the animation. Each token maps to a number in the model's vocabulary — typically 32K to 128K entries.

The word "unbelievable" becomes `["un", "believ", "able"]` — the model learns morphology by discovering common subwords during training.

## The Training Loop

Training an LLM is conceptually simple: show it text, have it predict the next token, measure how wrong it was, and nudge the weights to be less wrong.

This loop runs billions of times across terabytes of text. Each complete pass through the dataset is called an **epoch**.

<Aside title="What are scaling laws?">
Researchers at OpenAI showed that model performance improves predictably as you increase model size, data, and compute. Bigger models trained on more data with more GPUs = better results. This held true from GPT-2 to GPT-4, though recent focus has shifted to test-time compute — giving models more time to think during inference.
</Aside>

## Embeddings: Words as Vectors

Here's the really cool part. After training, words with similar meanings end up close together in a high-dimensional vector space.

The classic example: `king - man + woman ≈ queen`

<EmbeddingSpace
  words='[{"label":"king","x":120,"y":80,"group":"royalty"},{"label":"queen","x":280,"y":80,"group":"royalty"},{"label":"man","x":120,"y":200,"group":"gender"},{"label":"woman","x":280,"y":200,"group":"gender"},{"label":"prince","x":160,"y":140,"group":"royalty"},{"label":"princess","x":240,"y":140,"group":"royalty"}]'
  connections='[["king","queen"],["man","woman"],["king","man"],["queen","woman"]]'
/>

This isn't hand-coded — the model discovers these relationships from statistical patterns in text. The vector arithmetic works because the "gender direction" is consistent across the space.

## What This Means for Builders

Understanding these fundamentals changed how I think about:

- **Prompt engineering** — I now think in tokens, not words. Short precise prompts waste fewer tokens on noise.
- **Fine-tuning** — it's just resuming the training loop on domain-specific data. Same math, smaller dataset.
- **RAG** — retrieval-augmented generation works because embeddings make semantic search possible. Similar concepts = close vectors.
- **Context windows** — the attention mechanism is O(n²) with sequence length. That's why long contexts are expensive.

Next time you `await openai.chat.completions.create()`, you'll know what's happening under the hood.
